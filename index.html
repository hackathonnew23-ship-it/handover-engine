About this website

What Handover Engine is

A working tool that reads a teammate's scattered project activity and turns it into one handover the next owner can act on. It finds what is unfinished, what only one person knows and what access has to move, then gives every item an owner and a deadline.

Who it is for

  • Managers plan the handover, watch the readiness score and see which risks are still open.
  • Successors see what lands on them first, who owns each item and when it is due.
  • Leaving members capture what only they know, run the handover meeting and sign off.

What you get from one run

  • A risk-ranked packet of work, knowledge, access and decisions
  • A 3D graph of who holds what
  • A countdown plan with a calendar of do-by dates
  • A timed meeting agenda and a list of people to introduce
  • A last-day checklist with sign-off, plus Markdown and a short summary to share

The six result tabs

Packet

Every item grouped as work, knowledge, access or decision, with its owner, do-by date and a trace of the entries behind it. Filter by category, show risks only, tick items as resolved and change owners.

3D graph

The leaving member in the middle, items around them and teammates on an outer ring. Larger, pulsing nodes carry more risk. Drag to rotate, hover for details and click to jump to the item.

Plan

Days left until the last day, the four items to start with, a calendar of do-by dates, open items per owner and a chart of recent activity.

Meeting

A timed agenda with the riskiest items first and questions for each, the people to introduce, and check-ins 7, 30 and 90 days after the last day.

Checklist

Last-day tasks such as handing over access, rotating secrets and reassigning work, plus three sign-offs for the meeting, the successor and the leaving member.

Markdown

The whole packet as plain text for a doc, ticket or email. Copy summary gives a short message for a team channel.

Under the hood

  • Everything runs in your browser. The entries you paste or upload are not sent anywhere.
  • Plain rules read each line, link entries that share a PR or ticket number, match phrases such as "only I know" or "blocked", and score the risk. The same input always gives the same packet.
  • The 3D scenes use Three.js: the hero cube, the graph and Ember.
  • It cannot see knowledge that was never written down, and it only finds people whose names appear in the entries. The meeting agenda exists to draw out the rest.
Guide

How to run a handover

Use the generator in four steps, then follow the timeline so the packet turns into a real transfer of ownership.

STEP 1

Collect

Copy recent activity into the input box, one entry per line. A git log, a ticket export, chat notes and doc changes from the last 60 to 90 days work well. You can also upload a .txt, .csv or .json export.

STEP 2

Generate

Enter the leaving member, the successor and the last working day, then press Generate handover. The packet, graph, plan, meeting agenda and checklist all build from the same entries.

STEP 3

Review

Open an item's trace to see which entries it came from. Change owners, tick items that are already resolved, and use the filters to work through one category at a time.

STEP 4

Hand over

Hold the meeting from the generated agenda, work through the checklist, collect the three sign-offs, then copy the summary or the full Markdown packet to the team.

Entry format

One line per entry

[2026-10-02] PR #482 Dev Menon: 2 review comments open, waiting on Priya

What the four signals mean

Work in flight

Found from words like PR, WIP, blocked, waiting, failing, unassigned and due. It is flagged At risk when it is blocked, quiet for 14 days or more, or due before the last day. It is flagged No owner when it says unassigned or nobody else has touched it.

Knowledge only they hold

Found from phrases like only I know, nothing written down, outdated, manual workaround, in my DMs, who owns and ask followed by a name. The fix is a recorded walkthrough and a runbook entry.

Access to transfer

Found from passwords, tokens, keys, vault, admin roles and service accounts. It is marked Single holder when only one account has it. Do not paste secret values. Write where a secret lives, not what it is.

Decisions to preserve

Found from decided, agreed, going with, instead of and keeping. A decision stays marked Log it until an entry mentions a written decision record.

How the readiness score works

Readiness starts at 100 and loses points for each open item. It never drops below 5. Under 40 means critical gaps, 40 to 69 means needs work, and 70 or more means ready to hand over. Resolving an item removes its points. Giving an unowned work item an owner lowers its points from 9 to 3.

Work at risk6

Solo-held counts open items where nobody except the leaving member appears in the entries. These are the items most likely to disappear with them.

Timeline

  • 30 days beforeRun the generator and share the packet with the manager. Assign an owner to every item marked No owner.
  • 14 days beforeHold the handover meeting from the generated agenda. Start recording walkthroughs for knowledge items.
  • 7 days beforeThe successor works through the first items. Decisions are written up. The tool sets decision do-by dates here.
  • Last dayRevoke access, rotate shared secrets, forward email and collect the three sign-offs. Access items are due 2 days before, work 3 days before and knowledge 5 days before.
  • 7, 30 and 90 days afterCheck what the successor still had to ask, re-run the generator on their activity, and fix out-of-date runbooks.

Reading the 3D graph

The leaving member sits in the middle. Each handover item is a node around them, coloured by type. Bigger, pulsing nodes carry more risk. Teammates sit on the outer ring with a count of the open items they own, and lines run from each item to its owner. A ring with one person holding most lines is a single point of failure. Drag to rotate, hover for the item, click to open it in the packet.

Questions

Does my data leave the browser?

No. The analysis runs on this page and nothing you paste or upload is sent anywhere. Leave out passwords and key values anyway. The packet only needs to say where a secret lives.

How accurate is it?

It uses fixed rules, not an AI model, so the same input always gives the same packet. Every item has a trace of its source entries. Treat the packet as a draft to confirm in the handover meeting.

What does it miss?

Anything that was never written down. That is what the meeting agenda is for: each knowledge and access item comes with questions that bring the missing detail out of the leaving member's head.

Can I use it for more than one person?

Run it once per leaving member. Change the name and the last day, keep the entries, and the packet is rebuilt for that person.

What is on the roadmap?

Connectors that pull the same entries straight from GitHub, Jira and Slack, and an export that creates the checklist as tickets.

Method

How the packet is built

STAGE 1

Collect

Reads each entry and pulls out its date, source, person, references and any dates mentioned in the text.

STAGE 2

Link

Entries that share a PR or ticket number are merged into one thread, so a ticket, its PR and the review chat read as one story.

STAGE 3

Rank

Each thread is classified as work, decision, knowledge or access, then rated by risk: no owner, stalled, blocked, undocumented or single holder.

STAGE 4

Package

Produces owners, do-by dates, next steps and a readiness score that rises as items are resolved. Export it as Markdown.

Signals it detects

  • Work with no owner, no recent activity, open review comments or a due date before the last day
  • Knowledge that lives in DMs, personal notes or one person's head
  • Credentials, admin roles and keys held by a single account or a personal vault
  • Decisions made in chat that have no written record

Runs in your browser

  • Analysis is rule-based and runs locally on the page. Pasted activity is not uploaded anywhere.
  • Every item keeps a trace of the entries it came from, so each claim can be checked.
